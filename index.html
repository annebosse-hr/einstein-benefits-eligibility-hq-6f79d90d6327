Benefits Eligibility & Variable-Hour Strategy

Einstein currently makes every mover hired as "Full-Time" eligible for medical benefits after a 60-day wait, with no hours test at enrollment — but the BCBS contract itself only covers people averaging 30+ hrs/week, and Blue Cross's own definition is the ACA look-back standard. This project builds the data case for whether to move movers to a true ACA variable-hour classification (measurement period → stability period) instead, and if so, what measurement-period length and eligibility threshold make sense — ahead of the 2027 Open Enrollment renewal conversation with Paul.

✅ Resolved: the 20% vs. 43.5% ACA-clearance discrepancy

Same population, same ~123.8 hrs/mo mean, wildly different clearance rate between the 8/21 session (with Cali) and this pass. Tested both plausible methodologies directly against the Aug 2025–Jul 2026 dataset:
MethodologyResultMatches which figure?
Average hours across the full 11-month measurement period ≥130 — what the ACA look-back rule actually specifies114 / 262 = 43.5%This session's figure
Clears 130 hrs/mo in a majority of individual months (≥6 of 11)54 / 262 = 20.6%The 8/21 "~20%" figure
Clears 130 hrs/mo in every individual month (11 of 11)4 / 262 = 1.5%Neither — ruled out

The middle row is a near-exact match to "~20%" — the 8/21 pass most likely tested month-by-month consistency rather than the period average, which penalizes normal month-to-month volatility (mover hours run a stdev of ~34 on a mean of ~123) that the actual ACA rule doesn't test for. Use 43.5% going forward — it's the number the regulation itself specifies. Saved to memory (reference_aca_fulltime_hours_threshold) so this doesn't drift back to 20% in a future session.

✅ Investigated: why Garland and North Austin run low

Pulled current headcount, title mix, tenure, and status by branch to test what's actually driving this, rather than guessing.

Ruled out: trainee-heavy composition (North Austin's non-LIT Lead-level movers also average only 107.4 hrs/mo — well below South Austin's 136.8) and a general "bigger branch = fewer hours" pattern (headcount-vs-hours correlation across all 10 branches is weak, r=0.24 — South Austin and North Dallas are both large and run above-average hours).

Garland — likely churn, not a stable branch running low. 4 of its 7 Dec-2025 cohort have already left by Aug 2026 (57%, vs. 36% for North Austin and South Austin alike). Small (12-person crew today, opened 2021), young (75% hired in 2026, avg tenure under 10 months) — reads as still rebuilding its crew. n=7, directional not conclusive.

North Austin — two things worth chasing, no single clean answer. Two individual records look like data or leave-status anomalies: one Lead In Training shows 0.0 hrs/mo across all 24 tracked pay periods while still marked ACTIVE; another (now Regular Lead) averages 24.9 hrs/mo across the same full window — worth a leave-status check, not necessarily a branch problem. Removing those plus one churn case lifts the branch median from 115.8 to 117–123 hrs/mo — but even then, North Austin's typical mover still runs below South Austin's (median ~139.9). Turnover doesn't explain it (North Austin's 36% since-Dec-2025 termination rate exactly matches South Austin's). North Austin is the single largest crew in the network by a wide margin (60 active movers vs. a ~30-mover network average) — the remaining hypothesis is a job-volume-vs-crew- size mismatch specific to this branch, which needs a direct conversation with North Austin's branch manager or ops leadership to confirm — HR/payroll data can't settle it alone.

Decision needed

Open questions for Anne (and ultimately Paul)

These aren't agent tasks — they're calls only Anne and Paul can make.

1
Do we move movers to a true ACA variable-hour classification, or keep the blanket-FT model and bolt on an hours test? The current model (all FT-hired movers eligible after 60 days, no hours test) leaves ~150 currently-eligible movers below the BCBS contract's 30-hr/wk floor. Variable-hour reclassification fixes that but delays eligibility for new hires until they complete a measurement period — up to a year, depending on IMP length.
2
Measurement-period length: 6 months or 11–12 months? Quantified trade-off above: a 6-month IMP lets ~62% of eventual-leavers reach eligibility (vs. ~47% at 12 months) but also means more of them show up as a covered premium before they wash out. A shorter IMP also risks a slow-season hire never accumulating enough hours within the window; a 12-month IMP cascades over both a busy and slow season and is what the broker's example timeline assumes as the "standard" length for ongoing employees, though their worked example uses 6 months keyed to a Jan 1 launch.
3
Grandfathering: Option 1 or Option 2 (see broker guidance)? Option 1 keeps every currently-eligible mover covered and applies the new variable-hour rules only to new hires going forward — lower employee-relations risk, slower to close the carrier-contract gap. Option 2 runs current employees through a real measurement/transition period too — closes the gap faster but risks eligibility disputes with people who've had coverage for years.
4
Two North Austin records need an actual leave/status check. One Lead In Training shows 0.0 hrs/mo across all 24 tracked pay periods while still marked ACTIVE; another (now Regular Lead) averages 24.9 hrs/mo across the same full window. Investigation (above) narrowed this to a data/HR question, not a branch-health guess — worth a direct pull of these two people's actual status (LOA? stale record?) rather than more aggregate analysis.
5
Worth a conversation with North Austin's branch manager or ops? Even after removing the outlier records, North Austin's typical mover still runs below South Austin's — and North Austin is the largest crew in the network by a wide margin (60 active movers vs. a ~30-mover network average). This is a job-volume-vs-crew-size question HR/payroll data can't settle alone.
6
Is the 84-of-84 declined-without-valid-waiver finding a real gap? If a valid waiver is supposed to be on file for FT employees who decline coverage, this is a documentation gap worth folding into the same ETC conversation already underway for the ERISA wrap doc and Section 125 POP document.
